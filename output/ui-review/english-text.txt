                Sources and assumptions
                
                  i
                  Explain the real datasets and declared assumptions behind the platform's calculations.
                
              
              New territorial sources and assumptionsTerritorial criteria and coverage
The seven criteria retain their calculations and default weights: renewables 5, electricity infrastructure 4, water 5, connectivity 3, regulatory due diligence 4, environment 3 and substation 3. Water stress uses water; Energy capacity combines renewables, infrastructure and substation; Connectivity uses its own criterion; Social/territorial risk combines due diligence and environment. Each pillar uses only the rounded weighted average of its active criteria. There is no score, average or classification across pillars. A zero weight disables a criterion; a pillar with no active weights is not assessed. These are heuristics from 0 to 100, with higher always more favorable, not probabilities or scores issued by official agencies.

Substation: ONS, 1,684 facilities with coordinates and voltage ≥69 kV in the versioned dataset. Geodesic distance to the nearest facility: up to 20 km = 90; 20–50 km = 89–60; beyond 50 km within the radius = 59–40; outside these ranges = 32. Bonus of 5 for voltage ≥230 kV; no data = 40. This does not establish available capacity.
Infrastructure: versioned snapshot of 1,817 official geometries from ANEEL/SIGEL - ONS. Approximate distance to the nearest line segment within the radius: up to 5 km = 90; up to 20 = 75; up to 50 = 55; otherwise = 35. No line within the radius = 25; unavailable or partial query = 40. This is neither a connection route nor a capacity assessment.
Water, basin and climate: 30% of the previous WUI/sanitation indicator + 50% of the ANA microbasin class + 20% of the climate indicator. WUI still uses IT load, water-use intensity and state-level Aqueduct data; climatology does not change annual water consumption.
Microbasin: ANA/SNIRH, 2020 quantitative water balance, spatial query at the point. Low, medium/moderate, high, very high, critical and intermittent classes receive 90, 75, 50, 30, 15 and 20 points; no valid class = 50. Displays the code, region, class and percentage commitment. At a basin boundary, the most conservative returned class is used. Does not replace a water permit or an up-to-date water study.
Climate: NASA POWER/MERRA-2, gridded climatology with its period and query date shown in the interface. Uses climatological maximum T2M_MAX, humidity RH2M and precipitation PRECTOTCORR, not today's weather observations. Indicator = 100 − 4 × maximum temperature above 28 °C − 3 × months below 1 mm/day − 0.5 × mean humidity above 75%; bounded to 10–100. Missing/invalid data = 50. This is a thermal/water screening heuristic, not cooling-system design. INMET is not integrated yet.
Environment: MMA/CNUC, layer cnuc_26_07_31. Intersects the point with published polygons and identifies protected areas within the radius. A point in a strict-protection area = 15; in another protected area = 40; a complete query without point intersection = 70, without certifying the absence of other restrictions. If the service fails, municipal fallback is used (38 with registered strict protection; 58 otherwise). The nearest municipal seat is only a fallback reference and may belong to another municipality. Polygon quality depends on the source's declaration.
Regulatory due diligence: 20 for intersection with strict protection, 40 for another protected area, 60 otherwise. The engine links the location to consultation with the manager/management plan (SNUC), checking federal jurisdiction in a federal protected area other than an APA (LC 140/2011) and checking activity classification, land use and Law 15,190/2025. For own water abstraction/discharge, it recommends checking water permits. Rules reviewed on October 20, 2026; they do not determine approval, exemption or final jurisdiction. Zoning and specific state/municipal rules require external confirmation.

Coverage, alerts and map use
Intersections with protected areas and incomplete queries continue to generate alerts and due diligence regardless of weights. These warnings neither cap pillar scores nor represent automatic legal prohibitions. A score must not be interpreted as authorization. The social/territorial pillar has partial coverage: it is not a full assessment of social conflicts or territorial rights.
Official layers load when the point or radius changes. CNUC queries up to 400 features; if this limit is reached without confirming the end of the list, coverage is partial. Transmission uses the complete official inventory of 1,817 lines downloaded on October 20, 2026, with the acquisition date displayed. To keep the map responsive, up to 30 nearest protected areas and 40 nearest lines are displayed with simplified geometries; assessment uses the original queried geometries. The basin shown belongs to the point, not the entire resource area. The climate marker represents a grid reference, not a weather station.
Regional comparison uses preliminary local datasets and can be sorted by one identified pillar, initially Water stress. It shows all four values without an overall winner. The Site tab and point summary use detailed queries, so values may differ. Map markers do not encode scores; candidate selection and the initial choice are alphabetical. Each source shows its status and date. Changing location discards old responses; Refresh queries requests new data. Complete cached data can be reused for up to one hour when returning to a point; staying at the active point does not trigger automatic refresh. When retrying the same point and radius, a previous OK result may be retained as partial if the source fails; retention is not guaranteed on subsequent attempts.
FUNAI, INCRA, permanent preservation areas (APPs), buffer zones, embargoes, SGB risks and specific local rules have no geometry coverage in this version. Research remains a reference for expansion. Even with all four sources available, environmental clearance of the property must not be inferred. The system assesses a point and radius, without defining the property boundary or associated works.

              
        
            Point-and-radius data basis
            The four pillars in Summary and Site use resources within the radius, state-level WUI references, ONS substations and detailed territorial queries. Check each source's status in the Site tab.
            Regional comparison uses separate municipal/state indicators; it does not include these detailed queries and does not validate the point.
            Weights combine criteria only within each pillar. Setting all weights in a pillar to zero leaves that pillar unassessed, without hiding environmental or coverage alerts.
