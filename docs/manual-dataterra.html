How the four pillars are calculated

A practical guide to DataTerra's implemented data, formulas, evidence and limitations for preliminary data-center location screening in Brazil.

1. Purpose and interpretation

DataTerra presents four separate screening perspectives. It does not calculate an overall score, an overall suitability class or an average across pillars.

The practical question is: what resources and warning signs are visible around this candidate location, and what must be investigated before proceeding? The application supports initial comparison and technical due diligence, not a decision to approve construction.

1 · Water stress

How favourable are the implemented water-use, basin-balance and climate indicators?

2 · Energy capacity

What do nearby nominal renewable generation, transmission routes and substation records indicate?

3 · Connectivity

How close are the cable routes represented in the current inventory?

4 · Social/territorial risk

What conservation overlaps and preliminary regulatory questions are visible in the available records?

Each evaluated pillar is a heuristic value from 0 to 100; higher means more favourable under its formula. This direction also applies to the pillars named “Water stress” and “Social/territorial risk”: a higher number does not mean more stress or more risk. The separate WUI impact indicator has the opposite direction: lower impact is better.

The formulas are deterministic, use no machine learning and have not been empirically validated as predictors of site success, cost, approval, water security or emissions. An official input does not make DataTerra's derived value an official assessment. There are no pillar suitability bands or overall winner.

The application does not establish grid-access capacity, a water-supply commitment, a commercial fibre connection, land-use compatibility or legal clearance. It does not replace environmental licensing, water-use authorisation, engineering studies, property due diligence or consultation with affected rights-holders.

2. Using the application

  1. Select a state and a candidate municipality, or choose a point on the map. The selected coordinate is a reference point, not a delineated property.
  2. Set the search radius, IT load in MW and water-use intensity in L/kWh. These are scenario assumptions, not measured facility operation.
  3. Inspect resources within the radius and the four separate pillar values. Open the local analysis to examine the point-specific sources, warnings and assumptions.
  4. Review the source status, edition and query date before interpreting a value. A number can include a fallback even when some evidence is missing.
  5. Open the comparison tab and choose one named sorting pillar. Water stress is the default. Read all four values; ordering by one does not establish overall superiority.
  6. Adjust criterion weights only within their pillar. A weight of zero disables that criterion; disabling every criterion in a pillar leaves that pillar unevaluated.

The map uses neutral candidate markers, not colours or sizes derived from an overall rating. Candidate display limits and initial selection use alphabetical order rather than a hidden score. The selected candidate remains visible. Resource layers, the search circle and source-specific indicators remain aids to interpretation.

The theme control switches between light and dark and stores the preference in the browser. This manual uses a separate light print layout; the application's interface remains in Brazilian Portuguese.

3. Seven criteria, four independent calculations

The existing seven criterion calculations are retained. The grouping changes how they are presented and combined, not their individual formulas.

PillarCriterion and codeWeight

Conservation constraints · U (environmental)3

For a pillar g, include only its own criteria with positive weights. Let s be a criterion value and w its selected weight:

P(g) = round(Σ(w × s) / Σw), using active criteria in g only If Σw = 0: no evaluation (null; displayed as —)
With default weights Water stress = A Energy capacity = round((5E + 4T + 3S) / 12) Connectivity = C Social/territorial risk = round((4L + 3U) / 7)

Weights operate only within a pillar. Increasing the weight of A or C while it remains positive does not change its single-criterion pillar. Setting it to zero produces “—”, not zero and not a favourable assessment. In multi-criterion pillars, removing a criterion removes both its weighted value and its weight from the denominator.

The fixed 30%/50%/20% composition inside A is part of the existing water criterion; it is not a set of user-adjustable pillar weights. Missing source data and disabled weights are different: the formulas below retain their documented fallback values, whereas a pillar with no active weight has no evaluation.

No cross-pillar compensation: DataTerra does not sum or average the four values, assign an overall class, apply an overall ceiling, or use an overall score for ordering or map appearance. Conservation and incomplete-coverage warnings remain visible independently of weights. A favourable energy value cannot cancel a known territorial overlap.

Notation: round rounds to the nearest integer, with positive halves rounded up; clamp(x, a, b) confines x to [a, b]. Decimal points use English notation. Distances are in kilometres. Intermediate rounding explicitly described below is part of the implementation.

4. Pillar one · Water stress

Question: how favourable are the implemented water-use, basin-balance and climate indicators at this location?

Inputs actually consumed

WRI Aqueduct 4.0 · non-governmental
The code stores state-level baseline water-stress coefficients, identified as province baseline bws Tot, on a 0–5 scale, without acquisition metadata. It does not make a new Aqueduct query for each site. This is a broad state proxy, not a site or abstraction-basin measurement. WRI is an independent research organisation, not a Brazilian water authority.
Legacy SNIS water and sewerage records · government-origin attribution, limited payload provenance
The application consumes data/water.json, derived from the local Planilha_Pesquisa_Simplificada_AE2021.xls input. Records contain municipality/state, provider, service labels, a population field and associated municipal coordinates, but no acquisition date or source-edition field. The institutional reference is not a verified download URL for that workbook. Only service presence in the radius enters the local service indicator; this is not the current SINISA 2025 dataset.
ANA/SNIRH quantitative water balance, layer 47 · Brazilian governmental
A spatial query intersects the selected point with the 2020 basin-balance layer. Consumed attributes include COBACIA, UGRH, NORIOCOMP, CLASSE, BAL_PERC, DISP_Q95 and DEM_ACM. The class drives B; the basin code, region, river and commitment percentage provide context. The query date is not a new reference year for the balance.
NASA POWER / MERRA-2 climatology · United States governmental
The point API provides monthly T2M_MAX (°C), RH2M (%) and PRECTOTCORR (mm/day). The response's climatological period and query date are reported separately. This is a gridded climatology, not today's weather, a station on the property or a cooling design calculation. INMET station series are not integrated.

Water criterion A

A = round(0.30 × A₀ + 0.50 × B + 0.20 × K) Water stress pillar = A, while its weight is positive

A₀ combines the WUI-inspired suitability indicator and service presence; B represents the ANA basin class; K is the climate heuristic. A higher A remains more favourable, despite the pillar's “stress” name.

WUI-inspired impact and base indicator A₀

Let i be water-use intensity in L/kWh and e the stored Aqueduct stress coefficient for the state. The local calculation uses the state supplied with the point, or the nearest candidate region's state if none was supplied. This is not a point-in-state-boundary validation.

WUI impact = round(100 × clamp(i / 3, 0, 1) × (0.35 + 0.65 × clamp(e / 5, 0, 1))) WUI suitability = 100 − WUI impact A₀ = clamp(round(0.65 × WUI suitability + 0.35 × service indicator), 0, 100)

Water and sewerage points use municipal reference coordinates; they are not mapped intakes, treatment works, delivery connections or evidence of supply at the property. Provider population fields do not establish spare industrial capacity and may overlap across records.

The intensity presets are 0.3, 1.0 and 2.5 L/kWh. The calculation constrains i to 0–5 L/kWh; its use term saturates at i = 3. WUI impact classes are 0–25 low, 26–50 moderate, 51–75 substantial and 76–100 high. These are impact classes, not pillar or overall suitability classes.

An unknown state has e = 0 in the current code and is labelled as missing Aqueduct data. This fallback can understate impact; it is not evidence of low water stress. The indicator is inspired by The Green Grid WUI and WRI Aqueduct, not a certified reproduction of either methodology.

If more than one basin intersects the point, the returned basin with the lowest model value is selected. No mean is calculated over all basins in the radius. The 2020 classification is screening context, not a current abstraction entitlement, a flow measurement or an allocation to the proposed facility. The point's basin may differ from the basin of an eventual water-supply intake.

Climate component K

Let θ be the highest monthly T2M_MAX among the 12 months, H the arithmetic mean of the 12 RH2M values, and D the number of months with PRECTOTCORR < 1 mm/day. Here θ denotes temperature; T elsewhere denotes transmission.

K = round(clamp(100 − 4 × max(0, θ − 28) − 3D − 0.5 × max(0, H − 75), 10, 100))

All 12 months must be valid for each variable. Fill values and temperatures outside −70 to 65 °C, precipitation outside 0–100 mm/day or humidity outside 0–100% are rejected. Missing or invalid climate data give K = 50. H enters the formula before its display rounding. These thresholds are product heuristics, not engineering design limits.

Annual consumption: a separate scenario estimate

Annual energy (GWh) = round(IT load in MW × 8.76, 1 decimal) Annual water (ML) = round(i × IT load in MW × 1,000 × 8,760 / 1,000,000)

These assume continuous operation for 8,760 hours/year; the calculation constrains IT load to 1–500 MW. At 50 MW and 1 L/kWh, the estimates are 438 GWh/year and 438 ML/year. They do not add PUE or infer a cooling technology. Load changes volumes and renewable coverage, but not WUI impact directly. Basin and climate components change A without automatically changing i or annual water volume.

5. Pillar two · Energy capacity

Question: what do nearby registered renewable generation and mapped electrical infrastructure indicate for initial screening?

Inputs actually consumed

ANEEL generation inventory / SIGA · Brazilian governmental
The application uses data/energy.json, including plant name, type, nominal capacity in MW, municipality/state and coordinates. The current SIGA ingestion code selects renewable plants in operation and converts MdaPotenciaOutorgadaKw to MW. However, the published legacy payload has unpopulated phase and source-record-date fields and no acquisition date. The existence of the newer ingestion routine does not prove that its filters were applied to this snapshot, or that every published plant is currently operating.
ANEEL/SIGEL transmission, attributed to ONS · governmental publication of sector data
The territorial service reads a versioned snapshot of 1,817 line geometries, acquired on 20 October 2026, rather than downloading the live SIGEL inventory for every point. Geometry and line name are used for proximity and display. Acquisition time is not a certified update date for every asset.
ONS substation inventory · primary sector source, not a government agency
The versioned payload contains 1,684 records with coordinates and voltage of at least 69 kV. It includes identifiers, names, operator, voltage, coordinates and subsystem. Multiple voltage/bus records may refer to the same physical substation; this is not a count of 1,684 distinct installations. Its coverage is not all distribution infrastructure. The published array has no source-edition or acquisition-date field.
Energy capacity = round((5E + 4T + 3S) / 12) With custom weights, use only active E, T and S weights.

Renewable generation E

E = clamp(round(100 × renewable MW in radius / IT load MW), 0, 100)

Sum the non-negative nominal capacities of valid plant records in the radius; round the total to two decimal places before calculating E. Nominal capacity equal to the IT load already yields 100. No plant records in the selected inventory area yield zero, which does not prove that no generation exists physically.

MW are not available grid-access capacity. E does not model capacity factors, hourly coincidence, firm output, contracts, PUE, transmission constraints or a reserved connection. Nearby renewable generation is not proof that the facility will consume that generation.

Transmission proximity T

For a complete transmission response, d is the shortest distance to a line in the selected radius. The service rounds this distance to two decimal places before the following thresholds:

Transmission resultT

Distance is approximated on line segments, using 111.32 km per degree of latitude and a cosine correction for longitude. It is not a feasible connection route, an easement assessment or an electrical power-flow study. The calculation does not extract available connection MW from SIGEL popup text.

Substation proximity S

Select the nearest valid ONS record in the entire payload, not only those inside the radius. Use the unrounded Haversine distance d (Earth radius 6,371 km) and selected radius r. Apply these conditions in order:

S₀ = 90 if d ≤ 20 S₀ = clamp(round(89 − 29 × (d − 20) / 30), 60, 89) if 20 < d ≤ 50 S₀ = clamp(round(59 − 19 × (d − 50) / max(1, r − 50)), 40, 59) if 50 < d ≤ r S₀ = 32 otherwise S = clamp(S₀ + voltage bonus, 0, 100)

The bonus is 5 for voltage ≥230 kV, otherwise zero. It uses the selected record's voltage, not the maximum voltage of a deduplicated physical installation. If no valid substation is available, S = 40 without a bonus. The ≤20 km and ≤50 km conditions take precedence even when the substation lies outside a smaller selected radius; the voltage bonus also applies to S₀ = 32.

Neither substation proximity nor its voltage demonstrates spare capacity, bay availability, redundancy, connection cost or delivery date. A grid-access study and the applicable operator/distributor process remain necessary. Public access-capacity studies exist, but they are not consumed by these formulas.

6. Pillar three · Connectivity

Question: how close are the cable routes represented in the current inventory?

Input actually consumed

The application consumes data/cables.json, normalised from the repository's all_cables.json source. The inventory is attributed to TeleGeography, a non-governmental commercial source, and includes cable name, owners, reported year, length and route geometry. This publisher reference is not a versioned download URL for the shipped payload. The payload has no acquisition date or source-edition field and does not establish each route's current commercial status. A reported cable year is not a dataset refresh date.

Route geometry drives proximity; names and ownership provide context. Despite the legacy criterion name referring to “connectivity and market”, the local formula contains no economic market-demand variable. It does not consume Anatel backhaul or IX.br interconnection data.

Cable proximity C

Let d be the unrounded distance to the nearest valid cable geometry, including cables outside the selected radius. Let n be the number of cable records whose nearest geometry lies within the radius.

F(d) = 100 if d ≤ 5 F(d) = clamp(round(95 − 15 × (d − 5) / 20), 80, 95) if 5 < d ≤ 25 F(d) = clamp(round(79 − 29 × (d − 25) / 50), 50, 79) if 25 < d ≤ 75 F(d) = clamp(round(49 − min(29, 0.3 × (d − 75))), 20, 49) if d > 75 C = clamp(F(d) + 2 × min(n, 5), 0, 100)

If no valid cable geometry is found, F = 0 and C = 35; the bonus is not applied in that case. With a positive criterion weight, the Connectivity pillar is exactly C. Setting the weight to zero leaves it unevaluated.

Distance considers route segments and endpoints, with a local planar approximation for segments and Haversine point distances. A displayed distance rounded to one decimal place is not the input used for the thresholds. Each cable record counts once; the quantity bonus is not a count of independent ducts, carriers or delivery points.

A nearby submarine cable is not terrestrial fibre available for contract at the site. Route proximity and cable count do not prove a landing/access point, usable capacity, latency, commercial availability or physical redundancy. Those require provider quotations, confirmed delivery points and route-level due diligence.

7. Pillar four · Social/territorial risk

Question: what conservation overlaps and preliminary regulatory questions are visible in the available records?

Inputs actually consumed

The point analysis queries the governmental MMA/CNUC WFS, using MMA:cnuc_26_07_31, the declared 31 July 2026 edition. It intersects the point with published conservation-unit polygons and identifies units within the radius.

Consumed information includes unit name, group, category, administrative sphere, managing organisation, management-plan indication and polygon-quality field. The geometry establishes whether the point is inside a unit; the declared protection group selects the strict-protection case. A nearby unit outside the point does not by itself trigger that intersection case.

When the point query is not complete, the environmental fallback uses data/environmental-risk.json, derived from the MMA/CNUC July 2026 municipal CSV, without an acquisition date in the published array. Its strict-protection flag is matched by municipality name/state using the nearest municipal reference point, which can belong to a different municipality from the actual site. This is not a polygon intersection or a legal determination. The payload's Indigenous-land and quilombola fields are null, not evidence of absence; these sources are not integrated.

A known overlap takes precedence even if the query is partial. Nearby units are still relevant evidence for due diligence, but do not change these point-overlap values. Missing municipal information follows the 58 fallback; it does not establish absence of restrictions.

Social/territorial risk = round((4L + 3U) / 7) With custom weights, use only active L and U weights.

L and U share CNUC intersection evidence. Their combination is an existing modelling choice, not two independent confirmations. Higher values mean relatively more favourable screening conditions, not a probability of approval. This pillar has no direct measurement of social vulnerability, community impacts or the full set of territorial rights.

What the regulatory guidance means

  • For a conservation-unit overlap, consult the creation act, category, management plan and competent manager under Law 9,985/2000 (SNUC).
  • For a federal conservation unit other than an Environmental Protection Area (APA), verify federal licensing competence under Complementary Law 140/2011. The application raises a question for confirmation; it does not issue a binding competence ruling.
  • Confirm activity classification, size, associated works, local land use and the applicable licensing procedure, including Law 15,190/2025 and competent state/municipal rules.
  • For direct abstraction or discharge, verify water-use authorisation or a lawful exemption with the responsible authority; see ANA's water-use authorisation information. A modelled water volume does not establish an entitlement.

The implemented guidance was revised on 20 October 2026; that date is not a current legal opinion. The engine does not ingest all legislation or automatically determine approval, exemption, applicable zoning or the final licensing authority.

No CNUC overlap does not mean clearance. FUNAI and INCRA territories, permanent preservation areas, conservation buffer zones, embargoes, physical hazards and detailed local rules are not geometrically covered by this version. Protected-area data depend on the declarations and polygon quality of the source. The application evaluates a point and a radius, not the parcel, access roads, transmission works, water intakes or other associated interventions.

8. Coverage, distance and freshness

What a location and a radius represent

Municipal reference coordinates come from the non-official kelvins/Municipios-Brasileiros repository. The published payload contains name, state, latitude and longitude, without an IBGE identifier, boundary geometry, source version or acquisition date. It supports search, geolocation and nearest-reference fallbacks, not authoritative municipal containment or parcel boundaries. A circle around a point is a search window, not an impact footprint, service area or permitted resource catchment.

Point-to-point distances for plants, service records, municipal references and ONS substations use Haversine with an Earth radius of 6,371 km. Transmission and conservation-boundary distances use local segment projection with 111.32 km per latitude degree and a longitude cosine correction. Cable proximity also considers endpoints. These approximations do not include terrain, buildable corridors, road routing or cadastral precision.

Analytical coverage is not map display coverage

  • CNUC queries are limited to four pages of up to 100 features. Reaching 400 without evidence that the list has ended leaves coverage partial. Invalid or missing expected geometry also prevents a complete response.
  • The map displays at most 30 nearby conservation units and 40 nearby transmission lines, with simplified geometry. The proximity/intersection evaluation uses the original retrieved geometries, not just the visible subset.
  • The basin on the map is the selected point's basin, not all basins in the radius. Failure to obtain its display geometry does not invalidate already retrieved basin attributes.
  • The climate marker represents the gridded reference returned by NASA, not a meteorological station. Display geometry may also be omitted to keep a response within its size limit.

Source states and retained warnings

StateInterpretation
Complete / okThe implemented query completed under its checks. This is not certification of the source's accuracy, recency or legal completeness.
No data / no-dataThe query returned no applicable result. It is distinct from a failed request; the criterion's documented fallback still applies where defined.

“Four sources complete” refers only to CNUC, transmission, ANA basin and NASA climate. It does not validate the old generation, sanitation, cable, municipal or Aqueduct payloads, nor fill missing territorial layers. An unknown ANA class can arrive in an otherwise complete response and still receive B = 50.

Changing the point or radius changes the active query selection; late responses for an old selection are ignored. Successful results can be cached for up to one hour. The refresh control requests another application query, but does not rebuild the versioned transmission inventory or guarantee bypass of the server cache.

On a retry at the same point and radius, an immediately previous ok source result can be retained if the replacement fails; it is marked partial with a warning. Retained values are not a permanent history. Once marked partial, the record is not guaranteed the same preservation on another retry. This implementation detail must not be interpreted as durable evidence storage.

Source year, query time and publication date differ

Record the reference period of the subject matter, the publisher's edition and the acquisition/query timestamp separately. A query made today can still return ANA 2020 balance classes, a July 2026 CNUC layer or a stored transmission snapshot. Legacy payloads without adequate acquisition/version metadata must be treated as having limited provenance, not silently labelled current.

9. Regional comparison is not a point study

The regional comparison uses candidates built from municipal sanitation and generation records. It applies the same four-pillar membership and within-pillar weighting, but its input indicators are not all the same as the detailed local indicators.

Regional energy and transmission indicators are two transformations of generation capacity, not independent evidence of grid access. Candidates inferred from generation rather than sanitation have their own fixed/proxy rules. Regional water does not include the local service, basin or climate composition. These differences are intentional descriptions of the retained implementation, not methodological equivalence.

The retained regional proxy formulas

For auditability, let m be the sum of recorded renewable MW under the candidate's exact municipality/state key, s the corresponding state sum, and p the maximum population field among its sanitation records, not a sum of residents served. Define the logarithmic transformation R:

R(x; M, a, b) = clamp(round(a + (b − a) × log10(1 + max(0, x)) / log10(1 + M)), a, b)

Missing or invalid numeric inputs are treated as zero; R then returns a. These are proxy scales, not measured service or connection capacities.

Candidates backed by sanitation records

E = R(m; 1000, 55, 100) if m > 0; otherwise R(s; 1200, 35, 70) T = R(m; 1000, 50, 85) if m > 0; otherwise R(s; 2500, 45, 70) C = F(d) with a valid cable; otherwise R(p; 12000000, 42, 100) L = clamp(round(58 + 0.25q), 60, 82)

F(d) is the cable-distance function in chapter 6, without the local cable-count bonus and without a radius exclusion. The service proxy q is 78 when both separate exact source labels Água and Esgoto occur, 70 for water only, 64 for sewerage only, and 55 otherwise. Unlike local service parsing, the regional parser does not split a single combined Água e Esgoto label: that label alone follows q = 55. This retained parsing limitation can change L; it is not evidence of a real service difference.

Candidates inferred from generation only

These are created for municipality/state keys with valid energy records but no sanitation-backed candidate. Their coordinates are the arithmetic mean of plant coordinates, not a capacity-weighted centre. Sanitation-backed candidates likewise average the coordinates of their records.

E = max(60, R(m; 1000, 60, 100), min(100, round(m / 8))) T = R(m; 1000, 55, 88) C = F(d) with a valid cable; otherwise 52 L = 70

For both candidate types, A is replaced by 100 − WUI impact; it is not blended with the candidate's original water/service proxy. U is 38 when the matching candidate municipality's fallback flags strict protection, otherwise 58, including missing records. S uses the same nearest-ONS-record formula and selected radius as chapter 5. These seven effective values are then composed within the four pillars only.

Selecting a candidate triggers the point-based analysis; values can change when radius-specific resources and territorial queries replace the regional proxies. Do not present a regional comparison row as a completed site investigation.

Ordering is explicit: choose one pillar in the comparison control; the default is Water stress. The other three values remain separate. There is no total, overall class or cross-pillar tie-break based on a hidden total. A disabled pillar is unevaluated, not a zero-value or high-suitability candidate.

For a meaningful comparison, keep the scenario assumptions and weights consistent, identify the selected sorting pillar, and distinguish regional proxies from detailed point results in any exported notes or presentation.

11. Research context and important gaps

The repository's technical research maps useful sources and possible future work. It is a source-and-limits reference, not a description of everything integrated into this release. The following items explain the main gaps in the four current values; none is silently added to a formula.

Energy: national context is not a connection study

EPE's National Energy Balance, BEN 2025 has reference year 2024. It provides national/state context on production, consumption, installed capacity, renewable shares and emissions. It is not consumed by the local score formulas, is not a site-level connection-capacity source and cannot convert nearby nominal MW into deliverable power. National renewable shares are not a facility's hourly electricity mix or marginal emissions factor.

Public ONS access information and the research's 2026 PNAST remaining-capacity study show that some capacity information is publicly available. It is conditioned on study period, buses, works and assumptions; shared limits cannot simply be added, and the study does not reserve capacity. These studies and ANEEL's distribution database (BDGD) are mapped for future use, not current inputs to E, T or S.

Water: service records are not a supply guarantee

SINISA 2025, reference 2024, offers official service, losses and continuity indicators that are not in the legacy service-presence formula. ANA intake/supply-system information, monitored hydrological series and water-use authorisations can improve understanding of the actual source and competing uses, but are not integrated here. Neither municipal service coverage nor Q95 by itself is industrial supply available to this site.

INMET station histories are mapped for future climate work, not consumed alongside NASA. Future use would require representative stations, quality control and suitable periods, rather than assuming the nearest station supplies a design condition.

Connectivity: declared transport is not a physical route

Anatel's transport/backhaul data are an official source mapped for future use. The published links join municipal seats and do not represent physical fibre alignments; their geometry must not be used as a lot-to-fibre distance. IX.br localities and interconnection points, from NIC.br, are a primary non-governmental complement for commercial diligence, also not integrated. Neither source proves a free port, a contractible route, latency or independent redundancy.

Territory: conservation alone is not social/territorial coverage

FUNAI Indigenous-land information, INCRA quilombola-territory information and SGB susceptibility/risk mapping are mapped for future use, not intersected by this version. Legal stages, acts and coverage matter; no polygon or title in a dataset does not mean no rights. Areas outside mapped risk sectors are not automatically safe.

IBGE Census 2022 population and service data could inform affected-community context; IBGE Localities and official boundaries could support controlled geographic identifiers and joins. These are not a replacement already made for the current municipal-reference repository. Census localities do not define legal territories, and vulnerable or traditional populations must not be treated as an automatic penalty.

Protected-area buffer zones, management-plan zoning, permanent preservation areas, embargoes and specific local rules remain additional gaps. A future dataset's inclusion would require a deliberate implementation and methodological review; listing it in research is not integration or validation.

12. Reproducing and auditing a result

What to record

  1. Code version; whether the result is a regional proxy or a detailed local analysis; coordinate and reference municipality/state.
  2. Search radius, IT load, intensity in L/kWh, all seven criterion weights and the chosen regional sorting pillar.
  3. Seven criterion values, four pillar values or “not evaluated”, WUI impact, annual volumes and all warnings. Do not invent or record an overall total.
  4. Dataset editions, acquisition dates where available, query timestamps, returned source states and whether any earlier response was retained after a failed refresh.
  5. The actual responses/snapshots used, including geometry where relevant. Remote services and legacy payloads can change; the same coordinates alone are insufficient for reproduction.

Evidence boundary of this manual

The linked publisher pages and primary references identify source provenance and research context. Their inclusion does not report a new online availability check, a new data acquisition or an independent audit performed for this manual. Stored edition/acquisition dates are reported as declared by the implementation. Legacy payload provenance is less complete than that of the explicit territorial source metadata.

The formulas describe current behaviour, including fallbacks and approximations; they are not recommendations to treat missing evidence as adequate. The technical research's access observations and proposals must not be presented as production coverage. Any future formula or input change must be reflected consistently in the application, this HTML manual and its PDF.
